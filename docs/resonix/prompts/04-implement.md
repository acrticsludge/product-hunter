Read the five existing project skills first, at `.opencode/skills/source-discovery/SKILL.md`, `.opencode/skills/pain-point-extraction/SKILL.md`, `.opencode/skills/pain-point-normalization/SKILL.md`, `.opencode/skills/evidence-provenance/SKILL.md`, and `.opencode/skills/evidence-validation/SKILL.md`. House style, copied from those files:

- YAML front matter with exactly two keys, `name` and `description`.
- The description is one line, third person, and starts with "Use when". It states the trigger, not the topic.
- A single `#` title, then short sections with `##` headings.
- Tables wherever a decision has more than two branches.
- Dense, declarative sentences. No filler, no cheerleading, no emoji, no second person.
- A closing rule that fails closed, and a statement of what never counts.
- 40 to 60 lines. Cite the specification section the skill implements and the repository files that exist today.
- Never a claim without the evidence it needs. Deterministic ids, versions travelling with the record, and a rejection over a guess.

The prose rules from the repository's own agent instructions apply: no "In today's", no "delve", no "tapestry", no "not merely...but", no em-dash overuse, no forced rule of three, no binary contrast templates, and no uniform sentence lengths.

### 4.1 `source-reliability`

Implements specification §7 and the source-facing half of §12.

Must encode:

- The registry in `config/sources.yaml` is the only source of truth, loaded by `src/product_hunter/sources.py`. A source that is not in the registry does not exist.
- The fields that matter and what each one decides: `terms_status`, `allowed_use`, `reviewed_at`, `review_basis`, `robots_or_api_notes`, `collector`, `rate_limit_policy`, `domains`, `schemes`, `robots`, `enabled`.
- `require()` fails closed. A source is refused when it is unknown, disabled, its terms are neither `reviewed` nor `permitted`, it allows no use, or it does not grant the requested use.
- `analysis` is its own use. A source may allow retrieval and storage and still forbid reading the text with a model, and `ph extract` checks `analysis` for every fetch it selects.
- Reliability is a property of the registry entry plus observed behaviour, never a hand-set number. A reliability tier must name its basis: the terms status, the collector, the rate-limit policy, and the observed failure rate of the source in the run.
- A reliability tier is not a licence to widen the allowlist. Never widen the allowlist to let one fetch through.
- A source with a lower tier contributes less to any claim about a market, and a concentration report must be able to discount it. A single source cannot carry a market claim on its own.
- What never counts as a reliable source: a source whose terms status is `unknown`, an aggregator with no terms review of its own, a mirror of another source that carries no review of its own, and a source whose collector is `managed` before the Phase 7 gate.
- A zero result counts only when every required query family ran to completion. An unsearched family, a failed request, a blocked source, or a budget that stopped the run is not a zero.

### 4.2 `source-independence`

Implements specification §12.

Must encode:

- Independence is a computed property. It is never an LLM-provided number and never a number a report asserts.
- The graph shape: an author points at a `same-person-suspect` author; evidence points at `repost`, `quote`, `crosspost`, and `syndication` evidence.
- The author record fields: source or platform, stable handle or pseudonymous key, hashed identity key where appropriate, identity confidence, first and last seen, deletion status.
- The propagation edge fields: source evidence, derived evidence, propagation type, detection method, confidence.
- The seven computed numbers, in the order the specification lists them: raw mention count, canonical-content count, independent author count, uncertain-author count, independent community count, source concentration, propagation-adjusted evidence count.
- The discount rule. Suspected propagation and uncertain identity are discounted rather than counted as new people.
- The worked shape from the specification: 147 mentions, 91 canonical observations, 63 high-confidence independent authors, 8 uncertain authors, 12 communities, 4 propagation chains. The number that reaches a report is the propagation-adjusted one, and the report carries the rest as its denominator.
- Never claim an exact number of real-world people when the source only gives weak pseudonymous identity evidence. The uncertain-author count is reported next to the independent-author count, never folded into it.
- Fail closed: no independence number without the edges and the confidences that produced it stored alongside it.

### 4.3 `entity-resolution`

Implements specification §12 and the identity half of §14A.

Must encode:

- Two records are the same person only when the evidence supports it. Otherwise they stay separate records.
- The signals, and their weight: platform with handle, hashed identity key, display-name similarity, community overlap, cross-linking between accounts, and signature or phrasing reuse.
- Never merge on display name alone. A name is a weak signal and a merge on one is a defect.
- Every merge or suspect edge records the method that produced it and a confidence. A link below the merge threshold is a `same-person-suspect` edge, not a merge.
- Merges are reversible. The edge is stored so a split can be recomputed, and a later run that disagrees with a merge does not silently delete it.
- Identity keys are hashed. A raw identifier never reaches a report, an export, or an Obsidian note.
- A pseudonymous account is not a person until it is resolved, so it never enters the independent-author count as one.
- A deleted account is marked with a deletion status. It stays in the count history, because removing it would change a number that a past report already used.
- Fail closed: an unresolved author is reported as uncertain, and uncertain is never rounded up.

### 4.4 `problem-clustering`

Implements specification §14 and §14A.

Must encode:

- A cluster is a set of pain points that describe the same underlying problem, not a set of similar sentences.
- Clustering is versioned, and a prior cluster identity is an immutable historical record. The cluster fields are `id`, `version`, `merged_into`, `split_from`, `status`, `created_at`. The membership fields are `cluster_id`, `pain_point_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `assignment_confidence`, `clustering_run_id`.
- The Qdrant check is mandatory before a cluster is created and before a cluster is merged. The nearest-neighbour check decides, not the model's opinion.
- The merge test and the split test, stated as tests. A merge needs the members to describe one workflow with one consequence. A split needs each part to stand on its own as a problem.
- The normalized key from `norm-v1` is not clustering. The key groups exact wording only, so a paraphrase never forks a record and never merges two either. Embeddings find the candidate; a recorded decision makes the cluster.
- The pipeline, in order: normalize, embed, nearest-neighbour check, propose, verify, record with the assignment confidence and the run id.
- A cluster with one member is a candidate, not a problem, and it is reported as a candidate.
- Lineage. An opportunity stores the cluster version it was based on. When a cluster is merged, split, or superseded, the score stays attached to the historical cluster and a lineage edge points to the successor. No report may reinterpret an old score as belonging to a new cluster.
- Fail closed: no cluster without its members, its assignment confidence, and its clustering run id.

### 4.5 `frequency-analysis`

Implements specification §16 and the counting half of §12.

Must encode:

- Frequency counts independent occurrences. It never counts mentions, and the denominator is always stated next to the number.
- The three numbers kept apart: mentions, canonical observations, independent people.
- A count without a window is not a number. The window, the collection period, and the sources that ran travel with the count.
- Source concentration. A count dominated by one community is a property of that community, not a market rate, and the concentration number is reported next to it.
- Growth needs a baseline. A first observation is not a trend, and a run that stopped early is not a decline.
- The analytics layer computes, and the fixtures reconcile. A DuckDB count that disagrees with the SQLite records is a defect to fix, not a rounding difference to explain away.
- Coverage is part of the number. A report states which query families and which sources ran, so a low count and a zero can be read correctly.
- Never claim a market rate from a sample drawn out of one community, and never present a mention count as a people count.
- Fail closed: no frequency claim without its window, its denominator, and its coverage.
